Creative Patterns & Insights
What the data tells you about what works for Kohler D2C on Meta
WHAT'S WORKING
1

Static product-on-white outperforms lifestyle imagery

The Toobi Shower Faucet static (ROAS 3.11×) and Beam Kitchen Faucet static (ROAS 3.19×) are both clean product shots — no model, no kitchen set. The design sells itself. Kohler's product aesthetic is strong enough. Don't over-produce — clean, high-res, angle-lit product statics are the heroes.

2

Bundle framing drives video completion AND purchase

BraznToiletBundle has the best P100 completion (4.83%) AND 30 purchases at ROAS 1.74×. The creative explicitly shows the toilet + seat + flush together. This is the only video that combines a strong hook with a value proposition that converts. Extend this to: Shower bundle (showerhead + faucet + accessories), Kitchen bundle (sink + faucet + soap dispenser).

3

Same creative performs dramatically differently by audience quality

Citrus Handshower video: P25 completion = 18.4% in Lookalike vs 2.2% in AllProducts — an 8× difference on the same creative. The video is not the problem in AllProducts; the audience is. Before concluding a video has failed, check if it works in a higher-intent audience first.

4

ToiletCombos Static1 CTR (3.31%) vs Static3 CTR (2.57%) — variant hierarchy clear

Within the same campaign, Static1 is generating the highest CTR and most purchases. Static3 has 0 purchases on ₹5K spend. The algorithm is clearly already favouring Static1 but Static3 is still getting budget. Turn off Static3 and Static4, let Static1 and Static2 compete.

WHAT'S NOT WORKING
1

Carousel format is consistently underperforming — retire it

Avg carousel ROAS: 1.19×, CPP: ₹6,304. The Kitchen Faucet Carousel (310326) alone is ₹1L spend at 0.79× ROAS. Carousels require intent to swipe; Meta's feed rewards single-image or video thumb-stopping power. Replace all active carousels with static single-image or short-form video.

2

1-second average watch time on 3 videos = instant scroll-past

Citrus Handshower (AllProducts) and Strive Double Bowl in the AllProducts campaign both show avg watch time of 1 second. This means the first frame is not holding attention. All future videos need to open with product in use — not a brand fade-in or logo. First 2 seconds must answer "what is this and why do I care?"

3

ATC-to-purchase rate is only 13.2% — post-click funnel is leaking

3,065 ATCs produced only 405 purchases. That's 2,660 abandoned carts. The Toilet Bundle campaign has 353 ATCs but only 30 purchases (8.5%). Before scaling any budget, audit: (1) cart page load speed, (2) checkout steps, (3) payment method availability, (4) whether price is visible before checkout.

4

Sale creatives aren't differentiating the offer

BoldDays Static creatives (Emil, Atom, Malleco) are all the same product statics with presumably a price badge added. The EmilePullDown BoldDays is the only one performing (12.64× ROAS). The Atom and Malleco BoldDays variants are wasting ₹20K+ with zero purchases. A sale creative needs to show the magnitude of the saving, not just the product. "Up to 40% off" in the first frame changes everything.

📉 WHAT DROPPED DURING/AFTER SALE
1

Remarketing DPA collapsed from sale to post-sale (4.49× → 2.12×)

Sale urgency drove remarketing conversions hard. Post-sale, that urgency is gone and the same audiences are less responsive. Fix: create a "sale just ended" DPA creative with a soft CTA ("Still interested? Browse the full range") to retain warm audiences before they go cold.

2

StriveSingleBowl budget cut killed a 3.19× ROAS creative

This video was doing 3.19× ROAS during the sale. Spend dropped to ₹6.9K in P3 (from ₹40K in P2). With only ₹6.9K it couldn't generate meaningful purchases. This looks like a manual budget reallocation error — the creative itself was performing. Restore budget.

3

Kitchen Faucet Carousel deteriorated every period (0.90× → 0.64× → paused)

Was burning ₹57K in the pre-sale period at sub-1× ROAS. Budget continued into the sale at 0.64×. Finally paused post-sale — but ₹1L+ was already wasted. This is a creative that should have been caught before the sale window opened.

📈 WHAT PICKED UP AFTER SALE
1

StriveDoubleBowl: 1.74× (sale) → 5.07× (post-sale)

The sale drove 12 purchases from this video. Those buyers and browsers seeded a warm lookalike and remarketing pool. Post-sale, the algorithm found the exact people who had browsed Strive during the sale — and they converted at 5× ROAS. This is the sale-awareness flywheel working correctly.

2

ToobiShowerFaucet: 2.58× (sale) → 4.23× (post-sale)

Same pattern as Strive. Sale diluted ROAS slightly (more spend, broader audience, not all at sale intent), but post-sale the warm audience that discovered Toobi during the sale converted at premium ROAS. Keep running this creative — it compounds across periods.

3

BraznToiletBundle: 1.12× (pre-sale) → 1.71× → 2.37× (post-sale)

The only creative that improved in ROAS across all three periods. The bundle video's strong completion (4.83% P100) built brand recall during the sale. Post-sale buyers are returning to complete the purchase. This is the "video builds, static converts" flywheel — worth replicating for Showering and Kitchen Sinks categories.

Action for the next sale cycle: The pre-sale BAU period (Jun 1–5) had ROAS of 1.66× — the worst of all three windows. The Remarketing DPA was at 0.56× because it hadn't been reset after the pre-sale period exhausted its audience. Before the next sale, run a 2-week "audience warm-up": scale Lookalike DPA creatives, pause Remarketing DPA 3 days before the sale, reset the ATC window to 14 days, then relaunch DPA on Day 1 of the sale with fresh audiences. Expected lift: +1.5–2× ROAS on sale Day 1.
